\title[EMA-Det]{EMA-Det: Hardware-Aware Edge Verification for Two-Stage UAV Detection}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Enes Mert} \sur{Ayd{\i}n}}\email{23050211025@aybu.edu.tr}

\author[1]{\fnm{Osman} \sur{Toklu{\u{g}}lu}}\email{otokluoglu@aybu.edu.tr}

\author[1]{\fnm{Enver} \sur{{\c{C}}avu{\c{s}}}}\email{ecavus@aybu.edu.tr}

\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Department of Electrical and Electronics Engineering}, \orgname{Ankara Y{\i}ld{\i}r{\i}m Beyaz{\i}t University}, \orgaddress{\city{Ankara}, \country{T{\"u}rkiye}}}

\abstract{Autonomous detection of Low-Slow-Small (LSS) unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) on edge devices typically relies on two-stage pipelines in which a lightweight proposal stage nominates candidate regions and a verification network must evaluate them under strict computational and memory constraints. This paper presents EMA-Det, a hardware-aware region-verification network for this second stage, combining a physically initialized (Laplacian/Sobel) first layer with two early-exit gates that let high-confidence background regions bypass most layers. On a source-level-split, deduplicated dataset under a controlled $N{=}5$-seed, 150-epoch protocol, EMA-Det reaches $96.08\pm0.47\%$ recall, versus $73.75\pm1.86\%$ for a from-scratch and $78.17\pm0.57\%$ for a COCO-pretrained YOLOv8n baseline, indicating that mean average precision (mAP) alone is an unreliable indicator of verifier quality, since system-level recall $R_{\text{total}} = R_{\text{proposal}} \times R_{\text{verifier}}$ is bounded by the weaker factor. We also present a static hardware-resource analysis and a synthesized, resource-verified field-programmable gate array (FPGA) implementation (Zynq-7020) of the network's first block. Cross-sensor evaluation on the Anti-UAV410 thermal-infrared (IR) benchmark shows that the verifier trained on electro-optical (EO) imagery transfers zero-shot with precision intact but recall falling to $80.12$--$86.29\%$ and mAP@0.5 to $39.38$--$52.05\%$, while EMA-Det trained from scratch on thermal data reaches mAP@0.5 $89.52\pm0.93\%$ and recall $91.64\pm0.41\%$, consistent with genuine domain shift rather than an architectural ceiling.}

\keywords{LSS UAV Detection, Region Verification, Early-Exit, FPGA, Edge Computing, Cross-Sensor Generalization}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\textit{This manuscript extends a paper accepted at IEEE SIU
2026~\cite{aydin2026emadetconf} (proceedings pending) with a controlled
unified protocol (Sections~\ref{sec:cbam} and~\ref{sec:leakage}), a
decoupled spatial bounding-box (bbox) head (Section~\ref{sec:spatialbbox})
and a cross-sensor study (Section~\ref{sec:crosssensor}).}

Asymmetric threats posed by Low-Slow-Small (LSS) class unmanned aerial
vehicles (UAVs) to civilian and military infrastructure require a fast,
reliable ``sense-decide-act'' loop in autonomous
air-defense systems~\cite{luo2021threat,aydin2025aads}. Electro-optical (EO) sensors today
are typically 4K ($3840\times2160$) or higher; processing such streams
directly and in full-frame on edge devices using standard deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) is
infeasible in real time due to bandwidth and memory
constraints~\cite{qasaimeh2019comparing}. Fielded autonomous systems
therefore typically use a two-stage
cascade~\cite{kiefer2023roi}: a first stage (radar
priors~\cite{nabati2019rrpn}, acoustic sensors~\cite{tegler2025sound}, or
motion/background-subtraction) identifies candidate regions of interest (RoIs), and a second
stage crops small patches and feeds them to a network for verification.

Prior work commonly adapts general-purpose detectors (the YOLO family, SSD) — developed for full-frame optical/aerial
detection~\cite{li2020object} — to the RoI-verification
task~\cite{achmadiah2025energy,cao2023improved}. We argue, and
demonstrate empirically, that RoI verification is \emph{not} simply a
scaled-down detection problem: memory bandwidth is a more critical
edge-device bottleneck than raw
compute~\cite{qasaimeh2019comparing}, and edge-compressed YOLO variants
run into resource and energy--performance bottlenecks on field-programmable gate array (FPGA)-class
hardware~\cite{adiono2021low,zhang2022apply}. Full-frame architectures,
compressed into a micro-input regime, suffer spatial feature loss from
deep global-pooling and asymmetric data flow. Since total system recall
is $R_{\text{total}} = R_{\text{proposal}} \times R_{\text{verifier}}$,
a verifier's false negative is a system-level miss, making recall —
rather than mAP — a design-critical metric for the second stage (though not the sole
determinant of overall system performance).

\paragraph{What ``hardware-aware'' means here.} Architectural choices
follow the constraints of the target FPGA---on-chip block RAM (BRAM),
digital signal processing (DSP) slices and memory bandwidth---rather than
floating-point operations (FLOPs) alone: (i)~standard rather than
depthwise-separable convolutions, which raise bandwidth pressure on
BRAM-limited fabrics; (ii)~a streaming-friendly backbone whose first block
needs only a 3-row line buffer; (iii)~per-RoI early-exit gates for the
batch-1 verifier workload; (iv)~fixed-point arithmetic checked by
high-level synthesis. Only (ii) for Block~1 and (iv) are verified in
hardware (Section~\ref{sec:hardware}); (i) and (iii) are design
rationale.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (1) \emph{Recall-centred verifier design.} We
formalize RoI verification as the second factor of $R_{\text{total}}$ and
show, over $N{=}5$ seeds, that a full-frame detector (YOLOv8n, from
scratch or COCO-pretrained) with far higher mAP reaches markedly lower
recall than a small purpose-built network, so mAP alone is a poor selection criterion for
this stage (Section~\ref{sec:baselines}). (2) \emph{Background-aware early
exit.} Two confidence gates let background candidates skip most of the
network, cutting average FLOPs per RoI by 12.5--21.2\% for a recall cost
of 0.4--1.0 points (Section~\ref{sec:earlyexit}). (3)
\emph{Spatial-information-preserving box head.} Regressing the box from
the pre-pooling feature map explains most of the benefit that the
Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM)~\cite{woo2018cbam} appeared
to give; on top of it, CBAM costs recall (Sections~\ref{sec:spatialbbox}
and~\ref{sec:cbam}). (4) \emph{Hardware feasibility.} Block~1 is
synthesized and co-simulated on a Zynq-7020, with scaling limits and a
roadmap for the remaining blocks quantified (Section~\ref{sec:hardware}).
We also release a source-level-split, deduplicated dataset-construction
pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:leakage}) and measure EO$\to$thermal-IR
transfer (Section~\ref{sec:crosssensor}).

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Two-stage detection and efficient backbones.} Two-stage
detectors separate proposal from verification, trading latency for
accuracy vs.\ single-stage designs~\cite{ren2015faster}; cascades
refine localization through successive stages of increasing
intersection-over-union (IoU) threshold~\cite{cai2018cascade}. UAV-specific work follows the same
split~\cite{achmadiah2025energy,cao2023improved}; EMA-Det instead
targets the verification stage specifically, via
$R_{\text{total}} = R_{\text{proposal}} \times R_{\text{verifier}}$
(Section~\ref{sec:intro}) rather than as a scaled-down general detector.
Mobile backbones cut FLOPs via depthwise-separable/grouped
convolutions~\cite{howard2019mobilenetv3} and
channel shuffle~\cite{ma2018shufflenet}, but FPGA work shows these raise
memory-bandwidth pressure on BRAM-constrained
fabrics~\cite{zhao2024fpga}, consistent with EMA-Det's shallow,
standard-convolution backbone (Section~\ref{sec:baselines}). FPGA accelerator design is likewise shaped by the balance of compute
and memory bandwidth~\cite{zhang2015fpga}.

\paragraph{UAV and small-object detection.} Vision-based anti-UAV
detection and tracking has been benchmarked
extensively~\cite{zhao2022antiuav,jiang2021antiuav};
drone detection against a static background by motion cues and a CNN
classifier is studied in~\cite{seidaliyeva2020drone}, and the VisDrone
challenges benchmark detection and tracking on drone-captured
imagery~\cite{zhu2021visdrone}. Small-object detection in general is
addressed, e.g., by sliced inference~\cite{akyon2022sahi}. These works mostly evaluate full-frame
detection by mAP, whereas EMA-Det targets the verification stage of a
cascade and selects models by recall. Cross-modality UAV detection has been studied
as EO/IR fusion~\cite{sun2022dronevehicle} and as single-sensor thermal-IR
detection~\cite{antiuav410}; this paper fuses no modalities, and Section~\ref{sec:crosssensor}
quantifies the zero-shot EO$\to$thermal gap.

\paragraph{Early-exit inference and attention.} Confidence-gated early
exits let easy inputs skip later
stages~\cite{teerapittayanon2016branchynet,liang2024implementation},
surveyed in~\cite{rahmath2024earlyexit}, generally for classification
with no strong class prior; EMA-Det's gates (Section~\ref{sec:gates})
instead exploit the RoI-verification setting's background-majority
asymmetry. The Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) recalibrates channel and
spatial features at negligible cost~\cite{woo2018cbam}, and stride-free downsampling
preserves small-object spatial information lost to strided
pooling~\cite{sunkara2022spdconv}; Sections~\ref{sec:cbam}
and~\ref{sec:spatialbbox} reach a related conclusion via a decoupled
spatial bbox head rather than attention alone.

\section{EMA-Det: Architecture and Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{System-Level Latency Model}\label{sec:latency}
In a two-stage architecture, total system latency $T_{\text{total}}$ is
modeled as the proposal time plus the sum of per-candidate verification
times (Eq.~\ref{eq:latency}).
\begin{equation}
T_{\text{total}} = T_{\text{proposal}} + \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{RoI}}} T_{\text{verifier}}^{(i)}
\label{eq:latency}
\end{equation}
With a general-purpose full-frame detector as verifier, every candidate
patch, including empty sky, pays the full network's fixed cost.
EMA-Det's early exits keep the linear dependence on $N_{\text{RoI}}$ but
lower the average per-candidate cost $\bar T_{\text{verifier}}$:
$T_{\text{total}}\approx T_{\text{proposal}}+N_{\text{RoI}}\,\bar T_{\text{verifier}}$.

\subsection{Laplacian/Sobel-Initialized Kernel Bank}\label{sec:init}
At a constrained $128\times128$ resolution, small targets carry limited
spatial information. Standard random initialization leaves the network to
discover edge boundaries from data. The first convolution layer's weights $W_1$ are
instead initialized from a deterministic bank of Laplacian ($L$)
\cite{marr1980theory} and Sobel ($S$) \cite{kanopoulos1988design} operators:
\begin{equation}
W^{(0)}_{1,c} = \begin{cases}
K_c, & c \in \{1,\dots,K\} \\
R_c, & c \in \{K+1,\dots,C_{\text{out}}\}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:init}
\end{equation}
where $K_c$ are the deterministic physical priors ($L$, $L_2$, $S_x$, $S_y$,
and their signed sums), in the spirit of hand-designed filter priors in
CNNs~\cite{luan2018gabor}, and $R_c \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.1)$ is a random
term, as in standard random initialization~\cite{he2015delving}, that
preserves the layer's capacity to generalize beyond the fixed operators. These weights
are left unfrozen and are updated during training like any other layer.
The rationale is an inductive-bias argument: known edge operators give
a useful prior over local gradient structure, which should matter for
small targets at $128\times128$, where edges are a large share of the
signal. Section~\ref{sec:cbam} tests this against random initialization
and finds no measurable gain.
Figure~\ref{fig:featmaps} shows the resulting Block~1 responses: the
Laplacian and Sobel channels give clean edge maps of a UAV and of a
bird, and the trained kernels stay close to their priors (cosine
similarity $0.9998$ on average over the twelve fixed channels and five
seeds, minimum $0.9968$), i.e.\ the prior is retained rather than learned away. An untrained, randomly
initialized layer also responds to edges (last column), so the figure
illustrates the prior rather than proving a benefit.

\subsection{Architecture}\label{sec:arch}
Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the network. Block~1 (Section~\ref{sec:init})
reduces $1\times128\times128$ to $16\times64\times64$; Blocks~2--4
progressively reduce to $128\times8\times8$; Block~5 fuses two parallel
dilated branches ($d{=}1,2$) to $128\times4\times4$; Block~6 expands to
$256\times4\times4$; a verification head (global average pooling (GAP), dropout
$0.4$) produces a classification logit, and a separate spatial branch
(Section~\ref{sec:spatialbbox}) produces the 4-coordinate bounding-box
refinement.

CBAM~\cite{woo2018cbam} is \emph{not} part of the default forward path
(Section~\ref{sec:cbam}).

\subsection{Spatial Bounding-Box Head}\label{sec:spatialbbox}
Retaining spatial feature information for box regression, rather than
regressing from a globally pooled classification vector, is standard
practice in modern detection architectures~\cite{ren2015faster,liu2016ssd,lin2017focal,tian2019fcos}.
An earlier configuration pooled before both branches, discarding
spatial layout; here, classification stays on the pooled vector, while
the box head reads the pre-pooling $256\times4\times4$ feature map
(flattened into one linear layer). Table~\ref{tab:cbam}'s legacy row
reports a controlled $N{=}5$ ablation against the pooled-vector config:
the spatial head improves mAP@0.75 (mAP at an IoU threshold of 0.75;
+5.7 points) and mAP@0.5:0.95 (+2.9 points) at a cost of only 15K
parameters (${\sim}1\%$) and negligible FLOPs, while recall is
essentially unchanged and its cross-seed std decreases ($2.54\to0.47$);
it is the default throughout this paper.

\subsection{Loss Function}\label{sec:loss}
The verification head combines classification and box-regression terms
(Eq.~\ref{eq:loss}):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \lambda_{\text{cls}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\hat{p}, p) + \lambda_{\text{box}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{CIoU}}(\hat{b}, b)
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}$ is the binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss and
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CIoU}}$ is the Complete-IoU (CIoU) loss~\cite{zheng2020distance},
$\lambda_{\text{cls}}=1.0$, and $\lambda_{\text{box}}=2.0$. Early-exit gates
add their own BCE terms ($\lambda_{\text{gate1}}=0.3$, $\lambda_{\text{gate2}}=0.4$)
against the same binary label.

\subsection{Dynamic Early-Exit Gates}\label{sec:gates}
Following the confidence-gated early-exit paradigm
(e.g.\ BranchyNet~\cite{teerapittayanon2016branchynet}), Gates~1 and~2
(after Blocks~2 and~4) implement the threshold rule of
Eq.~\ref{eq:gate}: a candidate halts (classified background, no box
refinement) once its confidence falls below $1-\tau$; otherwise it proceeds.
\begin{equation}
\text{Result} = \begin{cases}
\text{Background (halt)}, & \hat{y}_i \le (1-\tau) \\
C_{i+1}(F_i)\ (\text{continue}), & \hat{y}_i > (1-\tau)
\end{cases}
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Dataset Construction}\label{sec:leakage}

The dataset is built from two public raw sources: rotary- and fixed-wing
LSS UAV photographs from a Kaggle collection~\cite{dasmehdixtr_kaggle}
(labeled, 80\% of the raw pool) and bird/empty-sky photographs from a
Roboflow Universe collection~\cite{mohsen_roboflow} (unlabeled hard
negatives, 20\%): 8{,}793 photographs in total (5{,}921 labeled UAV
candidates, 2{,}872 negatives). Both collections are web-aggregated and
include sequentially extracted video frames, so many photographs are
near-duplicates. To guarantee an uncontaminated evaluation, an
$8\times8$ average-hash comparison (Hamming distance $\le 5$, clustered
via union-find, one representative per cluster, preferring a labeled
member) is applied before patch extraction, reducing the pool to 2{,}142
unique labeled-candidate and 887 unique sky photographs, split into
train/val/test (70\%/15\%/15\%) \emph{before} cropping so every patch
inherits its source photograph's split, precluding source-level overlap.
One positive patch ($3\times$ context factor) and one hard-negative patch
(same photograph, near but not overlapping the UAV) are cropped per
photograph, yielding 4{,}136 patches (2{,}842/662/632 train/val/test),
each traceable to a unique source photograph. Conference-version
values~\cite{aydin2026emadetconf}, from a patch-level split without
deduplication, are superseded and should not be compared directly.

\subsection{Training Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
All models are trained with AdamW (weight decay $10^{-4}$), 5-epoch
linear warmup, cosine annealing, batch size 16, and gradient clipping
(norm 5.0), with early stopping (patience 20 on val.\ mAP@0.5, up to 150
epochs). All results, including the CBAM ablation, use $N{=}5$ seeds
$\{42, 7, 123, 2024, 31415\}$ under this identical protocol. Augmentation
uses flips, brightness/contrast jitter, motion blur, noise, small rotations and
coarse dropout, but not Mosaic/MixUp, which destroys small-target shape.

With the spatial bbox head, a paired $t$-test over the 5 matched seeds
finds no significant difference between configurations on mAP@0.5,
mAP@0.75 or mAP@0.5:0.95 ($p=0.89$, $0.77$, $0.83$). CBAM's remaining
cost is recall: $93.23\pm1.37\%$ vs.\ $96.08\pm0.47\%$ (paired
$t$-test, $p=0.017$), with a larger seed spread. Under the legacy
pooled-vector head, 2 of 5 CBAM seeds collapsed to 74--79\% recall (std
$8.85$), a collapse absent under the spatial bbox head (worst run
90.8\%), indicating that CBAM's benefit there substituted for spatial
information the pooling discarded. With that information restored, CBAM
adds no localization benefit and keeps a smaller recall cost, so the
final architecture omits it.

\textbf{Initialization.} The last row of Table~\ref{tab:cbam} repeats EMA-Det with a
random first layer (same seeds and protocol). Differences are within noise
(paired $t$-test: mAP@0.5 $p=0.98$, recall $p=0.93$), and validation
mAP@0.5 after 5, 10 and 20 epochs is $21.6/37.4/50.2\%$ with
Laplacian/Sobel versus $23.2/39.8/49.3\%$ with random initialization. The
prior thus gives no measurable gain in accuracy or convergence speed; we
keep it as an interpretable starting point, without a performance claim.

parameters, but lack EMA-Det's early-exit savings

\subsection{Hardware Feasibility Analysis}\label{sec:hardware}

A per-layer translation of the Block1$\to$Gate1$\to$Block4$\to$Gate2
path into synthesizable C++ for high-level synthesis (HLS; trained,
BatchNorm-folded weights, validated against PyTorch) is in
\texttt{hardware/}. A static bit-width analysis
(\texttt{src/estimate\_hls\_bram.py}) identified on-chip memory as the
main constraint of the Zynq-7020 (630~KB), Block~1 alone using 30.3\% of
it, which motivated a 3-row line buffer (768~B) in place of a
full-frame buffer. Block~1 was synthesized with Vitis Unified IDE 2025.2
(\texttt{xc7z020clg484-1}, 10~ns clock). C-simulation against the PyTorch
\texttt{Block1} reference gives a max.\ absolute difference of
$3.3\times10^{-6}$ (\texttt{float}) and $1.48\times10^{-2}$
(\texttt{ap\_fixed<16,6>}), consistent with input-quantization noise (the
accumulator uses \texttt{ap\_fixed<32,12>}). Table~\ref{tab:block1synth}
shows unquantized \texttt{float} overshooting the DSP and look-up table
(LUT) budgets by more than $3\times$/$2\times$, whereas
\texttt{ap\_fixed<16,6>} at native operand width uses $2.1\times$ fewer
DSPs at equal accuracy and fits every budget with $37\%$ DSP headroom.

C/register-transfer-level (RTL) co-simulation confirmed that the
generated RTL itself (not only HLS's C-simulation) reproduces this
result: \texttt{2/2} images passed at the same tolerance, with a
deterministic latency of 36{,}489 clock cycles ($\approx$365\,$\mu$s/image,
$\approx$2{,}740 images/s for this kernel alone). One layer is thus
verified numerically, by resource synthesis, and by RTL co-simulation.

This result covers only Block~1 (of six blocks plus two gates): it
fully unrolls every multiply--accumulate (MAC) operation (one multiplier
per MAC, initiation interval II$=1$), costing 145 DSPs for 144
MACs/pixel, affordable only with one input channel. Block~2's second
convolution needs $9{,}216$ MACs/pixel ($\sim$42$\times$ the DSP budget
under this strategy) and Block~6's $589{,}824$ ($\sim$2{,}680$\times$),
so Blocks~2--6 require a time-multiplexed MAC array (a systolic or
processing-element (PE) array reused over cycles).
It is not yet designed; the rest of the mapping is quantified but
unsolved. The roadmap below follows from the figures above.
(i)~\emph{Tiled PE array:} $T_m$ output $\times$ $T_n$ input channels in
parallel with $T_mT_n\le 220$ DSPs, so a layer takes
MACs$/(T_mT_n)$ cycles~\cite{zhang2015fpga}. For the remaining
$\approx$421M MACs (423.1M from \texttt{thop} minus Block~1's 2.4M), full
use of all 220 DSPs at one MAC per DSP per cycle bounds the latency below
at $\approx$1.9M cycles ($\approx$14\,ms at 137\,MHz, $\approx$72
images/s) before memory stalls.
(ii)~\emph{Weight streaming:} the 1.53M parameters need $\approx$3.1\,MB
at 16 bit ($\approx$1.5\,MB at 8 bit) against 0.63\,MB of BRAM, so the
weights of Blocks~2--6 must be streamed from external double-data-rate
(DDR) memory with double buffering, while activations stay in line
buffers as in Block~1; 8-bit quantization would halve this traffic at an
accuracy cost that must be measured. (iii)~Each block would repeat
Block~1's C-simulation $\to$ synthesis $\to$ co-simulation chain.

\subsection{Cross-Sensor Generalization}\label{sec:crosssensor}

To assess transfer to an unseen modality, an RoI verification dataset
was built from the Anti-UAV410 thermal-IR benchmark~\cite{antiuav410}
using its native video-level split (one video, \texttt{02\_6319}, spanned
both partitions and was excluded). Positive patches were cropped around
the annotated drone box ($3\times$ context, resized to $128\times128$);
negatives combine drone-absent frames with same-frame hard negatives, as
in the EO pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:leakage}). Every 15th frame was
sampled, yielding 27{,}768/12{,}388/16{,}808 train/val/test patches.

\textbf{Zero-shot transfer.} The EO-trained final checkpoint (spatial
bbox head, no CBAM) was evaluated on the thermal val/test splits without
fine-tuning. Precision stays high (95.31\% val, 95.53\% test), while
recall falls from the EO in-domain $96.08\pm0.47\%$
(Table~\ref{tab:cbam}) to 86.29\% (val) and 80.12\% (test), and mAP@0.5
from 58.03\% to 52.05\% and 39.38\% (mAP@0.5:0.95: 21.82\% and 15.77\%):
the learned features retain discriminative signal across modalities,
while EO/thermal silhouette shift accounts for a non-trivial fraction of
missed detections.

\textbf{Thermal-from-scratch reference.} To bound the zero-shot
result, EMA-Det (final) was trained from scratch on the thermal
train/val split under the identical protocol ($N{=}5$ seeds): mAP@0.5
$89.52\pm0.93\%$, mAP@0.5:0.95 $51.82\pm1.62\%$, recall
$91.64\pm0.41\%$, precision $97.55\pm0.39\%$. mAP@0.5 and precision exceed
both the zero-shot and the EO in-domain values; recall exceeds the
zero-shot values but stays below the EO in-domain $96.08\pm0.47\%$. The
same network (1{,}532{,}391 params, 423.085M FLOPs) thus reaches 89.5\%
mAP@0.5 when trained on thermal data, indicating domain shift rather than
an architectural ceiling.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
\textbf{Dataset size:} deduplication leaves 4{,}136 patches
(Section~\ref{sec:leakage}). \textbf{Cross-sensor
generalization:} one benchmark (Anti-UAV410); domain adaptation and
multi-sensor training were not evaluated. \textbf{Hardware validation:}
Block~1 only; the time-multiplexed MAC array for Blocks~2--6 remains
unsolved future work (roadmap in Section~\ref{sec:hardware}), and no
end-to-end on-board latency or power is reported. \textbf{Early exit:} it
costs 0.4--1.0 recall points (Section~\ref{sec:earlyexit}), and FPS is
measured on a GPU proxy. \textbf{Initialization:} the Laplacian/Sobel prior
shows no measurable gain over random initialization (Section~\ref{sec:cbam}). \textbf{Baseline pretraining:} COCO pretraining narrows but does not
close the YOLOv8n recall gap, and its cause is not isolated, so it cannot
be attributed to the architecture alone.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
This extended version reports EMA-Det under a controlled, $N{=}5$-seed
protocol on a source-level-split, deduplicated dataset. Decoupling the
box head from the pooled classification feature improves localization
and accounts for most of CBAM's earlier benefit, which substituted for
discarded spatial information. Comparison with YOLOv8n (from scratch
and COCO-pretrained) and lightweight backbones indicates that mAP alone is an unreliable
indicator of verifier quality, since recall, rather than mAP, bounds
system-level recall in a two-stage cascade. Early exit saves
12.5--21.2\% of FLOPs for 0.4--1.0 recall points. Cross-sensor
evaluation quantifies a genuine but non-catastrophic EO$\to$thermal gap,
and a synthesized, resource-verified first block, with a quantified
roadmap, scopes the work remaining for a full FPGA mapping. We release
the dataset-construction and evaluation tooling
(\texttt{build\_dataset\_clean.py}, \texttt{check\_leakage.py},
\texttt{check\_raw\_duplicates.py}) for reproducible construction of
similar datasets from web-aggregated imagery.
